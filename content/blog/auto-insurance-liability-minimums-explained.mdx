Every state that requires auto insurance sets a minimum amount of liability coverage drivers must carry.
That minimum is a legal floor, not a recommendation — it reflects what regulators consider the smallest
amount of protection acceptable to drive legally, not the amount that will actually protect your finances
after a serious accident.

## Why the minimum is often not enough

Liability limits are usually expressed as three numbers, such as 25/50/25: the first is per-person bodily
injury coverage, the second is per-accident bodily injury coverage, and the third is property damage
coverage, all in thousands of dollars. A serious multi-car accident can easily exceed a 25/50/25 limit,
leaving you personally responsible for the difference.

## What to consider beyond the minimum

Two factors matter most: what you could lose (savings, home equity, future wages) if you were found liable
for an accident beyond your coverage limit, and how much a higher limit actually costs, since liability
coverage is often inexpensive to increase relative to the added protection.

A licensed agent can walk you through current minimums in your state and help you think through how much
coverage makes sense beyond that floor.
